# Engine track plan

Scope: the simulation engine, lesson content, accessibility, verification and the
dissertation. The design track has the visual identity and the teacher
documentation.

Ordered by value to a real pupil or teacher, not by what is interesting to build.

## 1. Narrate the simulation for a pupil who cannot see it

The stated accessibility gap. Contrast, keyboard paths and reduced motion all
pass automated checks, but the 3D view is silent to a screen reader: the rover
drives, hits a rock, and nothing is announced. A blind pupil can write the
program and cannot tell what it did.

A polite live region announcing what actually happened, driven by the same run
events the grader counts, so the narration and the mark can never disagree.

## 2. New worlds and arenas

Grounded in what real educational robotics actually does (FIRST LEGO League,
VEX, RoboCup Junior) rather than decoration. The engine work is the same shape
either way: an arena is terrain plus obstacles plus bounds, and the lesson layer
already swaps all three.

## 3. Lesson packs

The Studio exports one lesson at a time. A scheme of work is ten. A pack is one
file holding many, so a teacher shares a term of work in a single send.

## 4. Whatever the market research ranks above these

A gap list is coming from seven parallel surveys of competitors, the DfE
curriculum, accessibility law, the pedagogy literature, teacher adoption and
simulation environments. If it names something more valuable than the above, it
wins and this plan changes.

## 5. Dissertation

Figures already match the shipped product at the tag. What is still owed is the
related-work chapter: the market survey gives it real citations instead of
assertions.

## Not doing, deliberately

Anything needing an account, a server, a paid service or a network call. Anything
that makes the product look more impressive without teaching better. A worse copy
of Scratch.
